## Smoking: the harm is well established

Tobacco smoke contains thousands of chemicals, including many known to cause cancer. Smoking is a major cause of lung cancer, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), heart disease and stroke. Second-hand smoke can also harm people around you.

In Australia, tobacco smoking contributed to **7.6% of the total disease burden in 2024**, according to the Australian Institute of Health and Welfare (AIHW).

The good news? **Quitting smoking improves your health at any age.** You do not need to wait until you develop symptoms, and it is never too late to benefit.

E-cigarettes heat a liquid to create an aerosol. Depending on the product and how it is used, that aerosol may contain:

- **Nicotine**, which is addictive.
- **Fine particles and metals** from the device.
- **Chemical compounds** produced by heating the liquid.
- **Flavouring chemicals** whose effects when inhaled may not be fully understood.

The contents and exposure levels vary between devices and products. A product that smells pleasant or tastes like fruit is not necessarily safe to inhale.

**1. Cough, wheeze and breathlessness**

Studies have reported more respiratory symptoms among people who vape. Some research has also found associations with asthma and other respiratory outcomes. However, smoking history and other exposures can make it difficult to determine how much risk comes from vaping itself.

**2. Acute lung injury**

A serious condition known as *e-cigarette or vaping product use-associated lung injury* (EVALI) was recognised during the 2019 US outbreak. Many cases were linked to THC-containing products and vitamin E acetate. This outbreak should not be assumed to represent every vaping product, but it demonstrates that some inhaled products can cause severe lung injury.

**3. Long-term disease**

Researchers are investigating possible links between vaping, COPD and cancer. We do not yet have enough long-term evidence to quantify the lifetime risks confidently. **Uncertainty does not mean there is no risk.**

## Is vaping safer than smoking?

These are two different questions:

- **Can nicotine vaping help some adults stop smoking?** Evidence suggests it can help some people quit.
- **Is vaping harmless, or proven safe for long-term lung health?** No. Its long-term effects remain uncertain.

Combustible cigarettes have well-established, serious health harms. But this does not make vaping harmless.

If you smoke, the priority is to stop smoking completely. Using both cigarettes and vapes may maintain exposure to tobacco smoke and nicotine; it should not be treated as a successful quit strategy.

In Australia, nicotine vaping products are regulated and may be considered in specific circumstances under current clinical guidance. They are not a general wellness product or a first-line choice for everyone. Discuss suitable options with a health professional.

You do not have to do it alone. A combination of support and treatment can make quitting more manageable.

Here are some evidence-based ways to get started:

- **Talk to your GP or pharmacist.** Nicotine replacement therapy (NRT), such as patches, gum or lozenges, and other suitable treatments can help manage cravings and withdrawal.
- **Get support.** Behavioural counselling can help you identify triggers, make a plan and stay on track.
- **Prepare for cravings.** Notice when you usually smoke, plan alternatives and ask people around you to support your decision.
- **Try again if needed.** Many people need more than one attempt. A setback can help you understand what support you need next time.

- **Online:** Visit [Quit.org.au](https://www.quit.org.au/) for practical advice and quitting resources.

You can also ask your GP about a plan tailored to your health, nicotine dependence and previous attempts to quit.

## The bottom line

Smoking causes serious, well-established harm. Vaping exposes your lungs to an aerosol that is not harmless, and its long-term health effects are still being investigated.

If you smoke, **getting help to quit is one of the most important steps you can take for your health**. You deserve practical support, not judgement.
